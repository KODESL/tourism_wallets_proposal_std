Executive summary

This document specifies the requirements for non-custodial tourism
wallets and presents them in a form intended for submission to
standards-development organisations (SDOs). It is the second Phase-1
deliverable (D1.2) of the StandICT.eu 2029 fellowship “Standardizing
Non-Custodial Wallet Infrastructure for Tourism” (Application
2029-01-1530), and it is derived directly from the state-of-the-art
survey and gap analysis in the companion deliverable D1.1.

The document does three things. It restates the sector use cases in a
formal, testable form (section 6). It specifies twenty atomic
requirements, written in RFC 2119 normative language and grouped into
functional, operational, security-and-privacy, and trust-and-lifecycle
categories, each with a rationale, a source use case, the gap it closes,
a verification criterion, a priority, and a target standardisation venue
(section 7). And it organises those requirements into ready-to-submit
packages addressed to specific bodies and work items — W3C, ISO/TC 307
(including ISO/TS 23516 and ISO/AWI 7603) and JTC 1/SC 27 JWG 4, ETSI TC
ESI and ISG PDL, the EU Digital Identity / eIDAS expert process, the
OpenID Foundation and ISO/IEC 18013 work, and the Ethereum standards
process (section 9).

Five requirements are flagged as the Phase-2 priorities, corresponding
to the highest-leverage gaps identified in D1.1: a standardised binding
between the credential and ledger-token representations of an
entitlement (TW-F-03), a wallet-portable expression of time- and
venue-bound validity (TW-F-04), offline verification at a defined
assurance level (TW-NF-01), a tourism data-minimisation profile
(TW-SP-01), and alignment of tourism attestations with the EU Digital
Identity Wallet (TW-TL-04). To keep the document usable by both
W3C/IETF-style and ISO/ETSI-style processes, Annex B maps the RFC 2119
key words used here to the verbal forms of the ISO/IEC Directives.

1\. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

The purpose of this document is to express, precisely and verifiably,
what a non-custodial tourism wallet and its supporting credential and
entitlement formats must do, and to package those requirements so that
they can be contributed to the relevant standardisation efforts with
minimal reshaping. It is a requirements specification, not a design: it
states what is required and why, not how a particular product implements
it.

1.2 Scope

The scope is the wallet, credential and entitlement layer for tourism:
how a traveller holds and presents tourism rights and identity
attributes in a wallet they control, and how an operator, venue or
authority verifies those rights. Consensus mechanisms, payment-scheme
specifics, token economics and the internals of ticketing or
property-management systems are out of scope except at their interfaces
to the wallet. The requirements are technology-neutral: they reference
existing standards as targets and alignment points but do not mandate a
specific ledger or vendor.

1.3 Relationship to D1.1 and Phase 2

D1.1 surveyed the state of the art and identified fourteen gaps (G1–G14)
against twelve high-level requirements (R1–R12). This document refines
those into twenty atomic, normative requirements (TW-…), each traceable
back to the D1.1 gaps and to the use cases. The submission packages in
section 9 correspond to the Phase-2 plan: they define the artifacts
(use-case contributions, requirements inputs, profile drafts and
technical notes) that will be taken into the working groups during Phase
2 (Standards Contribution and Technical Drafting).

1.4 Structure of a requirement

Each requirement in section 7 is presented with a stable identifier and
title, a single normative statement, and the following attributes:
Rationale (why the requirement exists); Source & gap (the use case it
derives from and the D1.1 gap it closes); Verification (an objective
criterion by which conformance can be judged); Priority (Must, Should or
Could, with Phase-2 priorities flagged); and Target venue (the SDO and
work item best placed to take it forward). Identifiers use the prefix
TW- (tourism wallet) followed by a category code: F (functional), NF
(non-functional/operational), SP (security and privacy) and TL (trust
and lifecycle).

1.5 Conformance and key words

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, REQUIRED, SHALL, SHALL NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD
NOT, RECOMMENDED, MAY and OPTIONAL in this document are to be
interpreted as described in RFC 2119 and RFC 8174 (BCP 14) when, and
only when, they appear in capitals. Because several target bodies (ISO,
ETSI) draft requirements using the verbal forms of the ISO/IEC
Directives Part 2 (“shall”, “should”, “may”), Annex B gives the mapping
so that each requirement can be reformatted for the receiving body
without changing its meaning. Priorities (Must/Should/Could) follow the
MoSCoW convention and are distinct from the normative key words: a
requirement may be normatively expressed with SHOULD yet be prioritised
as a Must for the fellowship’s purposes, or vice versa.

3\. Terms and definitions

For the purposes of this document, the following terms and definitions
apply. Abbreviations are expanded on first use elsewhere in the
document.

| **Term** | **Definition** |
|:---|:---|
| Non-custodial wallet | A wallet in which the holder alone controls the keys, credentials and entitlements; no third party holds custody. |
| Holder | The traveller who holds and presents entitlements and identity attributes from their wallet. |
| Issuer | An operator, venue or authority that issues signed entitlements or attestations and may revoke them. |
| Verifier (relying party) | A venue, inspector or service that requests and verifies a presentation. |
| Entitlement | A tourism right held in a wallet — a ticket, reservation, pass, access right or loyalty balance. |
| Verifiable Credential (VC) | A tamper-evident, issuer-signed set of claims (W3C VC Data Model 2.0). |
| DID | Decentralized Identifier (W3C) — a controller-provable identifier independent of a central registry. |
| Electronic attestation of attributes (EAA) | An eIDAS attestation about a person or entity; QEAA is its qualified form. |
| Selective disclosure | Disclosing only the attributes a verifier requires for a stated purpose. |
| Redemption | The act of using an entitlement (e.g. gate entry), recorded so that its state can be verified. |
| Trust registry / trust list | A published, verifiable source establishing which issuers or verifiers are legitimate. |

4\. Actors and roles

The requirements assume the following actors. A single legal entity may
play more than one role (for example, an attraction may be both issuer
and verifier), but the roles are kept distinct because the requirements
differ by role.

| **Actor** | **Role and responsibilities** |
|:---|:---|
| Traveller (Holder) | Holds entitlements and attributes; controls keys; consents to and performs presentations; may hold entitlements for a group. |
| Issuer | Issues signed entitlements/attestations; defines their validity and constraints; revokes or suspends them; is discoverable via a trust registry. |
| Verifier (Relying Party) | Requests a presentation for a stated purpose; verifies signature, validity, status and trust; records redemption where applicable. |
| Wallet provider | Supplies the wallet software and recovery features; never holds custody of the holder’s keys or credentials. |
| Trust registry / anchor | Publishes verifiable information about legitimate issuers and verifiers (e.g. eIDAS trust lists, sectoral registries). |
| Ledger / network | Optional substrate where token-form entitlements and redemption or status data may be recorded. |

5\. Reference model

The reference model combines the W3C three-party model (issuer – holder
– verifier) with the three-layer view established in D1.1. Requirements
are located deliberately at the seams between the layers, because that
is where the gaps lie.

| **Layer** | **Provides** | **Representative standards** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Identity & trust | Who and what is trusted; identity attributes; revocation; eIDAS-grade trust | W3C DID & VC 2.0; eIDAS 2.0 / EUDI; ETSI EN 319 4xx |
| Exchange | How credentials are issued and presented; selective disclosure; offline retrieval | OpenID4VCI/VP; SD-JWT VC; ISO/IEC 18013-5 |
| Ledger / entitlement | Programmable entitlements; transfer; on-chain redemption and status | ERC-20/721/1155/4907/6672/5192; ISO/TS 23516 |

A tourism entitlement may be expressed in the identity-and-trust layer
(as a verifiable credential or attestation), in the ledger layer (as a
token), or in both. The requirements that follow do not force a choice;
instead, several of them require that the layers be bound and verifiable
together (notably TW-F-03).

6\. Use cases

The four use cases from D1.1 are restated here in a formal structure
(actors, preconditions, trigger, main flow, postconditions) so that
requirements can be traced to them. They are representative, not
exhaustive.

6.1 UC-A — Multi-entry city attractions pass

**Actors.** Issuer (city pass operator), Holder (traveller, with
family), Verifier (attraction staff).

**Preconditions.** The traveller has a non-custodial wallet; the
operator is listed in a trust registry.

**Trigger.** The traveller purchases a three-day multi-attraction pass.

Main flow:

1.  The operator issues the pass to the wallet as a verifiable
    entitlement carrying a validity window and the set of eligible
    venues.

2.  At each attraction the traveller presents the pass; the verifier
    checks the validity window, venue scope and current redemption
    state.

3.  Where connectivity is poor, the verifier evaluates the presentation
    offline against locally cached issuer trust data.

4.  The pass is bound to the holder and family and cannot be resold;
    group members are admitted under the holder’s presentation.

5.  Entry is recorded so any per-venue entry limits can be enforced.

**Postconditions.** Entries are recorded; the pass remains valid until
the window expires.

**Requirements exercised.** TW-F-01, TW-F-04, TW-F-06, TW-NF-01,
TW-NF-02, TW-TL-03, TW-SP-04.

6.2 UC-B — Cross-border multi-day transport entitlement

**Actors.** Issuer (regional transport authority), Holder (traveller),
Verifier (inspector across operators and borders).

**Preconditions.** Cross-border trust data is available to inspectors’
devices.

**Trigger.** The traveller obtains a multi-day, multi-operator transport
entitlement valid across two Member States.

Main flow:

6.  The authority issues the entitlement with coded zone and
    validity-window constraints.

7.  An inspector requests a presentation on a handheld device, sometimes
    offline on a moving vehicle.

8.  The entitlement verifies against cross-border trust data; its coded
    semantics are language-independent.

9.  The inspector confirms the validity window and zone scope.

**Postconditions.** A privacy-preserving record that the entitlement was
checked.

**Requirements exercised.** TW-F-02, TW-F-04, TW-NF-01, TW-NF-03,
TW-SP-03, TW-SP-04.

6.3 UC-C — Hotel check-in with minimal identity disclosure

**Actors.** Issuer (identity/credential issuer; booking platform),
Holder (guest), Verifier (hotel front desk).

**Preconditions.** The guest holds identity attributes and a reservation
entitlement; the hotel can check issuer trust.

**Trigger.** The guest arrives to check in.

Main flow:

10. The hotel requests confirmation of identity, legal age and a valid
    reservation, for the stated purpose of check-in.

11. The wallet presents only the minimal attributes: a name match, an
    age-over assertion and the reservation entitlement.

12. The hotel verifies issuer legitimacy via an eIDAS-aligned trust
    mechanism.

13. No full identity document is copied or stored.

**Postconditions.** Check-in is completed with minimal personal data
retained.

**Requirements exercised.** TW-F-07, TW-NF-03, TW-SP-01, TW-SP-03,
TW-TL-04.

6.4 UC-D — Cross-border festival with group tickets

**Actors.** Issuer (festival organiser), Holder (group buyer), Verifier
(gate and on-site benefit points).

**Preconditions.** The buyer holds the group’s tickets; resale
constraints are defined.

**Trigger.** The group arrives at the festival.

Main flow:

14. One buyer receives the group’s tickets in their wallet, each bound
    to a beneficiary.

15. At the gate, tickets are redeemed and the redemption state updates.

16. The same tokens later unlock on-site benefits through additional
    redemptions.

17. Resale is constrained; any “already used” dispute is resolved from
    privacy-preserving evidence.

**Postconditions.** Redemption states are recorded; benefits are
granted; audit evidence is available.

**Requirements exercised.** TW-F-03, TW-F-05, TW-F-06, TW-NF-02,
TW-TL-03, TW-SP-05.

7\. Requirements

Twenty atomic requirements follow, grouped into four categories. Each is
traceable to one or more use cases and to a D1.1 gap. Five are flagged
“Must (Phase-2 priority)”, corresponding to the highest-leverage gaps.

7.1 Functional requirements

**TW-F-01 — Entitlement representation**

**Requirement (MUST).** A non-custodial tourism wallet MUST be able to
hold tourism entitlements — tickets, reservations, access rights and
loyalty — as issuer-signed, verifiable objects that are independent of
any single operator’s platform.

**Rationale.** Entitlements today are locked into proprietary platforms,
which prevents portability and raises integration cost for SMEs.

**Source & gap.** UC-A, UC-C, UC-D; closes gap G11 and contributes to
G1.

**Verification.** A wallet holds an entitlement issued by one operator
and presents it for verification without that operator’s application.

**Priority.** Must.

**Target venue.** W3C Verifiable Credentials Working Group; ISO/AWI
7603.

**TW-F-02 — Shared entitlement semantics**

**Requirement (SHOULD).** Entitlement types and their attributes SHOULD
be expressed using a shared, machine-readable vocabulary so that meaning
is preserved across issuers, operators and Member States.

**Rationale.** Cross-border verification fails silently when semantics
differ even where formats agree.

**Source & gap.** UC-B, UC-C; closes gap G11 and contributes to G5.

**Verification.** An entitlement’s type and attributes resolve to the
same semantics for a verifier in another Member State.

**Priority.** Should.

**Target venue.** ISO/TC 307 (ISO 22739 revision); W3C.

**TW-F-03 — Credential–token binding**

**Requirement (MUST).** Where an entitlement is represented both as a
verifiable credential and as a distributed-ledger token, the entitlement
model MUST support a standardised binding enabling a verifier to confirm
that both representations refer to the same entitlement, without
mandating a specific ledger.

**Rationale.** This seam between the credential and ledger layers is the
single highest-leverage gap identified in D1.1.

**Source & gap.** UC-D, UC-A; closes gap G1.

**Verification.** A verifier validates either representation and
cryptographically confirms the two refer to the same entitlement.

**Priority.** Must (Phase-2 priority).

**Target venue.** W3C Credentials Community Group; ISO/TS 23516; ETSI
ISG PDL.

**TW-F-04 — Time- and venue-bound validity**

**Requirement (MUST).** An entitlement MUST carry machine-evaluable
validity constraints (time window, venue or scope) that a verifier can
evaluate deterministically and, where required, offline.

**Rationale.** Tourism rights are intrinsically time- and place-bound;
current credential models do not express this portably.

**Source & gap.** UC-A, UC-B; closes gap G3.

**Verification.** A verifier accepts or rejects a presentation solely
from the presented constraints, including offline.

**Priority.** Must (Phase-2 priority).

**Target venue.** W3C Verifiable Credentials Working Group; aligns
ERC-4907; ISO/TS 23516.

**TW-F-05 — Redemption semantics**

**Requirement (MUST).** The entitlement model MUST support interoperable
recording and verification of redemption events, including multi-step
redemption, so that a verifier can determine the current redemption
state.

**Rationale.** Gate check-in and multi-benefit passes require a
redemption record that is not tied to one ecosystem.

**Source & gap.** UC-D; closes gap G4.

**Verification.** A multi-redeemable entitlement reports correct state
across two redemptions performed at different verifiers.

**Priority.** Should.

**Target venue.** ETSI ISG PDL; W3C Credentials Community Group; aligns
ERC-6672.

**TW-F-06 — Group and delegated holding**

**Requirement (SHOULD).** A holder MUST be able to hold and present
entitlements bound to other beneficiaries (for example, a family), and
SHOULD be able to delegate presentation to another holder, with
verifiable linkage between holder, beneficiary and entitlement.

**Rationale.** Group and family travel is routine; the single-holder
assumption in current models does not fit.

**Source & gap.** UC-A, UC-D; closes gap G6.

**Verification.** One holder presents entitlements for several
beneficiaries in a single interaction, each independently verifiable.

**Priority.** Should.

**Target venue.** W3C VCWG/CCG; EU Digital Identity Wallet (requirements
input).

**TW-F-07 — Selective presentation**

**Requirement (MUST).** Presentation MUST support selective disclosure,
allowing the holder to present only the attributes a verifier requires
for a stated purpose.

**Rationale.** Data minimisation is both a privacy duty and a user-trust
requirement.

**Source & gap.** UC-C; contributes to gap G8.

**Verification.** A holder satisfies an age check without disclosing a
full date of birth.

**Priority.** Must.

**Target venue.** W3C; SD-JWT VC (IETF); ISO/IEC 18013-5.

7.2 Operational and non-functional requirements

**TW-NF-01 — Offline verification**

**Requirement (MUST).** Verification MUST be possible to a defined
assurance level when there is no network connectivity at the point of
presentation.

**Rationale.** Gates, vehicles and remote venues frequently lack
reliable connectivity.

**Source & gap.** UC-A, UC-B; closes gap G2.

**Verification.** An entitlement verifies on an offline device using
only locally available data, to the stated assurance level.

**Priority.** Must (Phase-2 priority).

**Target venue.** ISO/IEC 18013 work; OpenID Foundation; tourism
requirements via W3C and ISO/TC 307.

**TW-NF-02 — Verification performance**

**Requirement (SHOULD).** Verification SHOULD complete within a defined
latency budget under sustained peak arrival rates, and MUST NOT require
per-presentation network round-trips whose cost scales with queue
length.

**Rationale.** Peak-load gates fail when verification latency or
connectivity dependence grows with the queue.

**Source & gap.** UC-A, UC-D; closes gap G5.

**Verification.** Measured verification latency stays within the agreed
target at the defined peak throughput.

**Priority.** Should (numeric target to be set with operators).

**Target venue.** ETSI; ISO/TC 307.

**TW-NF-03 — Cross-border interoperability**

**Requirement (MUST).** An entitlement issued under one Member State’s
scheme MUST be verifiable, unchanged, under another’s, using
language-independent (coded) semantics.

**Rationale.** Trips cross jurisdictions within days; re-issuance per
border is not viable.

**Source & gap.** UC-B, UC-C; closes gaps G7 and G11.

**Verification.** An entitlement issued in Member State A verifies in
Member State B without re-issuance.

**Priority.** Must.

**Target venue.** EU Digital Identity Wallet / eIDAS; ETSI TC ESI;
ISO/TS 23516.

**TW-NF-04 — Accessibility**

**Requirement (MUST).** Presentation and verification flows MUST meet
the accessibility expectations of the European Accessibility Act,
including non-visual alternatives to any visual verification step.

**Rationale.** Accessibility is a legal requirement and a precondition
for equitable access to tourism.

**Source & gap.** All use cases; closes gap G13.

**Verification.** A presentation can be completed without reliance on
sight.

**Priority.** Should.

**Target venue.** ETSI / CEN-CENELEC accessibility work; EU Digital
Identity Wallet alignment.

7.3 Security and privacy requirements

**TW-SP-01 — Data-minimisation profile**

**Requirement (MUST).** A tourism presentation MUST disclose only
attributes within an agreed minimal set for the stated purpose, and the
ecosystem SHOULD define minimal attribute sets per common purpose (for
example, venue entry or age check).

**Rationale.** Selective disclosure exists technically but, without
agreed minimal sets, minimisation is left to each implementer.

**Source & gap.** UC-C; closes gap G8.

**Verification.** A documented minimal set exists for each purpose, and
presentations conform to it.

**Priority.** Must (Phase-2 priority).

**Target venue.** ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 27 – ISO/TC 307 JWG 4; W3C.

**TW-SP-02 — Limited correlation**

**Requirement (SHOULD).** Repeated presentations of the same or related
entitlements SHOULD NOT enable correlation of the holder across
unrelated verifiers beyond what is necessary for the stated purpose.

**Rationale.** Tourism involves many short interactions; naive designs
make travellers trivially trackable.

**Source & gap.** UC-A, UC-B; contributes to gaps G8 and G14.

**Verification.** Two presentations at different venues are not
trivially linkable by those verifiers.

**Priority.** Should.

**Target venue.** JWG 4; W3C (e.g. batch issuance, status-list privacy).

**TW-SP-03 — Issuer and verifier trust establishment**

**Requirement (MUST).** A verifier MUST be able to establish the
legitimacy of an issuer, and a holder SHOULD be able to establish the
legitimacy of a verifier, through an eIDAS-aligned trust mechanism that
scales to the large population of tourism issuers and verifiers.

**Rationale.** eIDAS trust lists cover qualified trust service
providers, not the sector’s many operators and venues.

**Source & gap.** UC-B, UC-C; closes gap G7.

**Verification.** A verifier confirms issuer legitimacy via a trust list
or registry without prior bilateral setup.

**Priority.** Must.

**Target venue.** ETSI TC ESI; EU Digital Identity Wallet / eIDAS.

**TW-SP-04 — Revocation and suspension**

**Requirement (MUST).** Issuers MUST be able to revoke or suspend an
entitlement, and verifiers MUST be able to check status in a timely and
privacy-preserving manner, including offline-tolerant status where
feasible.

**Rationale.** Lost, refunded or fraudulent entitlements must be
stoppable without leaking holder identity.

**Source & gap.** UC-A, UC-B; contributes to gaps G2 and G14.

**Verification.** A revoked entitlement is rejected, and the status
check does not reveal the holder’s identity.

**Priority.** Must.

**Target venue.** W3C (Bitstring Status List); JWG 4.

**TW-SP-05 — Auditability and dispute evidence**

**Requirement (MUST).** It MUST be possible to produce
privacy-preserving evidence that an entitlement was or was not used,
sufficient to resolve a dispute, without exposing unnecessary personal
data.

**Rationale.** Disputes over redemption are common in events and tourism
and must be resolvable fairly.

**Source & gap.** UC-D; closes gap G14.

**Verification.** An “already used” dispute is resolved from evidence
that reveals no additional personal data.

**Priority.** Should.

**Target venue.** JWG 4; W3C.

7.4 Trust and lifecycle requirements

**TW-TL-01 — Non-custodial control**

**Requirement (MUST).** The holder MUST retain sole control of the keys
and credentials in the wallet; no other party may exercise control over
them except under an explicit, holder-granted, revocable authorisation.

**Rationale.** This is the defining property of the model and the basis
of user data sovereignty.

**Source & gap.** All use cases; underpins requirement R8.

**Verification.** No issuer, verifier or wallet provider can present or
transfer an entitlement without the holder’s authorisation.

**Priority.** Must.

**Target venue.** ISO/AWI 7603; ISO/TC 307 (security work).

**TW-TL-02 — Recovery without custody**

**Requirement (MUST).** The wallet MUST provide a means to recover
access to entitlements after device loss that does not transfer custody
of keys or credentials to a third party.

**Rationale.** Travellers lose devices; a scheme that strands them will
not be adopted, but recovery must not reintroduce custody.

**Source & gap.** UC-A; closes gap G9.

**Verification.** A user recovers their entitlements after device loss,
with no third party having held custody at any point.

**Priority.** Must.

**Target venue.** ISO/TC 307 (security); Ethereum account-abstraction
process (ERC-4337 profile).

**TW-TL-03 — Consumer-protection controls**

**Requirement (MUST).** The entitlement model MUST allow expressing and
enforcing transfer and resale constraints (including
non-transferability), and MUST be able to represent refund, withdrawal
and cooling-off states.

**Rationale.** Consumer law and anti-scalping needs require controllable
transfer and clear refund states.

**Source & gap.** UC-A, UC-D; closes gap G12.

**Verification.** A non-transferable entitlement cannot be validly
presented by an unauthorised holder; a refunded entitlement is
verifiably void.

**Priority.** Should.

**Target venue.** W3C; ETSI ISG PDL; aligns ERC-5192.

**TW-TL-04 — EU Digital Identity Wallet alignment**

**Requirement (SHOULD).** Tourism attestations SHOULD be expressible as
electronic attestations of attributes compatible with the EU Digital
Identity Wallet and verifiable by EUDI relying parties.

**Rationale.** The EUDI Wallet is the European centre of gravity;
tourism attestations are natural attestations of attributes but are not
yet profiled.

**Source & gap.** UC-C, UC-B; closes gap G10.

**Verification.** A tourism attestation is issued and verified through
an EUDI-compatible flow.

**Priority.** Must (Phase-2 priority).

**Target venue.** EU Digital Identity Wallet / eIDAS expert group; ETSI
TC ESI.

9\. Submission packages by standards body

This section organises the requirements into packages addressed to
specific bodies and work items, with the suggested form of each

contribution plan. The same requirement may appear in more than one
package where it sits at a seam between bodies; in those cases the
packages should be coordinated through liaison.

- **Requirements.** TW-F-01, TW-F-02, TW-F-03, TW-F-04, TW-F-06,
  TW-F-07, TW-SP-02, TW-SP-04.

10\. Open issues for SDO discussion

The following are deliberately left open. They are not omissions but
questions whose resolution requires the consensus of the receiving
bodies, operators and data-protection authorities, and they are offered
as discussion items for Phase 2.

- **O-1 Non-custody versus recoverability.** Which recovery models (for
  example, social recovery or threshold schemes via account abstraction)
  are acceptable while preserving the non-custodial property in
  substance, not only in form? (TW-TL-01, TW-TL-02.)

- **O-2 Transferable versus non-transferable.** What should the default
  transfer model be, and how should consumer-protection constraints be
  expressed so they are enforceable yet not anti-competitive?
  (TW-TL-03.)

- **O-3 Offline assurance levels.** A tiered model of offline
  verification assurance is needed, defining what guarantees hold
  without connectivity and for how long. (TW-NF-01, TW-SP-04.)

- **O-4 Performance targets.** Concrete latency and throughput targets
  must be set with operators for representative peak scenarios.
  (TW-NF-02.)

- **O-5 Minimal attribute sets.** Minimal disclosure sets per purpose
  should be co-defined with operators and data-protection authorities.
  (TW-SP-01.)

- **O-6 Trust-list scaling.** A mechanism is needed to establish trust
  for the very large, dynamic population of tourism issuers and
  verifiers across borders. (TW-SP-03.)

11\. Conclusion and next steps

This document converts the analysis of D1.1 into a structured, normative
requirements set that can be contributed to standardisation with minimal
reshaping. The twenty requirements are atomic, individually verifiable,
and each is routed to a specific body and work item. Five priority
requirements concentrate the fellowship’s Phase-2 effort on the
highest-leverage gaps.

The immediate next steps for Phase 2 are: to prepare the W3C use-case
and credential-profile contribution and the credential–token binding
note (TW-F-01/03/04); to submit the interoperability and privacy inputs
to ISO/TC 307 and JWG 4 (TW-F-03, TW-SP-01); to open the
offline-verification profile discussion with the OpenID Foundation and
the ISO/IEC 18013 community (TW-NF-01); and to bring the tourism
attestation profile to the eIDAS expert process (TW-TL-04). The open
issues in section 10 will be tabled as discussion items, and the
open-source reference implementation planned for Phase 2 will be used to
demonstrate the priority requirements and to provide evidence for the
contributions.

Annex B — RFC 2119 to ISO verbal-form mapping

Requirements in this document use RFC 2119 / BCP 14 key words. When a
requirement is contributed to a body that drafts to the ISO/IEC
Directives Part 2, the key word should be substituted as below, without
changing the requirement’s meaning.

| **RFC 2119 / BCP 14** | **ISO/IEC Directives Part 2** | **Meaning** |
|:---|:---|:---|
| MUST, REQUIRED, SHALL | shall | Absolute requirement |
| MUST NOT, SHALL NOT | shall not | Absolute prohibition |
| SHOULD, RECOMMENDED | should | Recommendation; deviations permitted with justification |
| SHOULD NOT, NOT RECOMMENDED | should not | Discouraged; deviations permitted with justification |
| MAY, OPTIONAL | may | Permission; truly optional |

*End of document.*
